TEACHING EXCERPT
The recommendations called for coordinated tracking of federally funded opioid initiatives and for programs to use measurable goals. They proposed a national curriculum for opioid prescribers, participation data for continuing education, and training for pharmacists evaluating the legitimacy of opioid prescriptions.

The document also recommended stronger prescription-drug-monitoring programs, interstate data sharing, electronic prescribing, integration of monitoring data with health records, and improved real-time surveillance. Other recommendations concerned medication disposal, treatment access, overdose reversal, law enforcement, and data standards.

This policy document helps identify proposed oversight mechanisms. It does not show whether the proposals were adopted or whether they changed prescribing, treatment access, or overdose outcomes.
